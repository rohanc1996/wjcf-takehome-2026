# D6: Note to leadership

**Subject: Where AB PM-JAY cards are not turning into hospital care**

We compared district-level enrolment against hospital use in the synthetic extract: do districts whose usable hospitals are mostly private use the scheme less once enrolment is held constant? This is a scripted pipeline on synthetic data mirroring the scheme files, so it carries over to the NHA's real data.

**What we found.** In private-dominated districts, card holders raise about 0.9 approved hospitalisations per card, versus about 1.3 in comparable public-dominated districts, and the gap holds across enrolment levels. Use tracks who owns the hospitals, not how many cards.

**Where to look first.** District 11 is the clearest: no usable hospitals and no approved claims despite high enrolment, so supply is the constraint in this case. Elsewhere, districts 8, 20, 17 and 18 pair high enrolment with low use and thin or private-heavy supply.

**What we would not claim.** The data is synthetic and deliberately dirty, so these findings point to where to look, not to true prevalence, and are not proof that private ownership causes lower use. We do not quote coverage above 100%: district 7's is inflated by duplicate records, district 13's by an understated eligible-population estimate.

**The approach is a model for real data:** auditable cleaning and a one-screen result, rerun on the NHA's files as needed.

**Privacy.** If these were real claims, the summaries would be safe to share, but the claim records would need strict access controls and de-identification under the DPDP Act and SAHI.
