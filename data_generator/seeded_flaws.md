# Seeded flaws

All data is generated by `generate_data.py` with Faker and a fixed seed (42). Rows: 20 eligible-population records, 6314 beneficiary rows, 178 facilities, 13033 claim rows.

## Scale (why the numbers are small)

Real Indian districts average about 1.8 million people (range 50k to 10m+) and about 2700 hospital beds, which we take to mean roughly 170 facilities per district. Those averages are far too large for a synthetic extract with a few thousand beneficiaries and claims, so each synthetic district is modelled as a small reference unit:

- Eligible population per district: uniform 350 to 700 (total about 10.5k), so that at realistic enrolment (35% to 95%) the card-holder total stays in the few-thousand range.
- Facilities: 4 to 14 per district, average about 9, total 178 (meets the brief's minimum of 150). Facility density is compressed relative to real India, so "per 100k eligible" figures are meaningful only within this synthetic frame, not against national statistics.
- The synthetic district does not map to a real administrative unit.

## Claim status split

Roughly 55% approved, 23% rejected, 22% pending (measured: 7145 / 3024 / 2864 of 13033). Only approved claims count as use.

## Coverage above 100% in two districts, for different reasons

| District | Rows | Unique ids | Eligible | Coverage | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | 295 | 238 | 280 | 105.4% | Duplicate or fraudulent beneficiary entries. 57 beneficiary_id values appear twice in beneficiaries.csv. These should be flagged, de-duplicated, and batch-analysed for fraud patterns, not treated as real enrolment. |
| 13 | 310 | 310 | 230 | 134.8% | eligible_population is understated. Plausible real-world causes: the denominator comes from stale 2011 census age-group projections, and some states (e.g. Maharashtra) top up the central PM-JAY criteria with their own funds, widening who is eligible. Here the right fix is to refresh the eligible-population figure from a more recent source and cross-check with state-level expansion data, not to de-duplicate. |

## Design choices

- 20 districts across 4 states, 5 districts each.
- eligible_population is a separate reference file (the denominator for coverage). It is not a column in any other file.
- Facility supply is deliberately skewed: districts 3, 11 and 17 have very few facilities, and district 11 ends up with zero empanelled-and-active hospitals.
- The private share of empanelled-active supply is varied across districts (roughly 0.0 to 1.0) and drives the engineered signal: districts dominated by private supply generate fewer approved claims per card. Coverage is random and independent of supply, so the analysis must control for it.
